# Design research for the second editorial pass

This note records the public academic websites and design commentary reviewed before the September 2026 revision. It is a rationale for future edits, not a template to copy literally.

## Patterns worth keeping

- **Lead with a coherent line of inquiry.** Andrew Ibrahim's [Surgery Redesign](https://www.surgeryredesign.com/) makes its purpose clear before presenting individual outputs. For this site, the equivalent is a quieter question-led focus on treatment, surgery and recovery rather than a large personal mission statement.
- **Organise research into intelligible themes.** The judges in [The Academic Designer's 2025 review](https://theacademicdesigner.com/2025/winners-of-the-best-personal-academic-websites-contest-2025/) repeatedly praise sites that connect projects to related outputs, state takeaways and distinguish current from completed work. The Research page therefore uses three questions that can later hold projects, publications and presentations.
- **Keep the public structure concise.** [Ana Kamšek](https://kamsekar.github.io/) uses a short navigation and groups work around a clear research focus. Leadership remains part of Bodhi's narrative, but it does not need its own top-level destination at this stage.
- **Use colour as editorial structure.** [Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel](https://mine-cr.com/) uses a broad, quiet colour field; [Julia Silge](https://juliasilge.com/) uses one stronger accent around a portrait. This site follows that restraint with warm paper, oxblood and a mineral blue used for page openings and one homepage focus band.
- **Make teaching resources genuinely useful.** Ibrahim's public guides and the education-focused examples in the 2025 review treat resources as part of academic work, not as an ornamental blog. The Resources page therefore stays small, labels its template honestly and only introduces search and filtering once the library is large enough to need them.

## Choices deliberately not emulated

- Publication counts, testimonials, impact metrics or claims of expertise.
- Patient-facing marketing language or appointment-style calls to action.
- Stock operating-theatre imagery, surgical instruments or anatomical decoration.
- A separate page for every CV category.
- Large personal slogans, elaborate motion or app-like interfaces.

## Editorial test for future additions

Before adding a page, section or feature, ask:

1. Does it help a visitor understand the questions Bodhi works on, the way he teaches or the outputs he has produced?
2. Is the information verified and useful enough to justify permanent navigation space?
3. Can it be expressed more clearly inside an existing page?

If the third answer is yes, prefer the existing page.
