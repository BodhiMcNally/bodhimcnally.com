# Design research and editorial direction

The public examples reviewed before this revision included [Andrew Ibrahim's Surgery Redesign](https://www.surgeryredesign.com/), [Ana Kamšek](https://kamsekar.github.io/), [Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel](https://mine-cr.com/), [Julia Silge](https://juliasilge.com/) and the [Academic Designer's 2025 review](https://theacademicdesigner.com/2025/winners-of-the-best-personal-academic-websites-contest-2025/).

The useful common ground was not a particular layout. It was restraint: short navigation, a clear account of current work, outputs that are easy to find and teaching material that is useful in its own right.

## Current editorial rules

- Identify Bodhi with direct facts before discussing interests or ambitions.
- Use literal page names such as About, Research and Teaching rather than headline slogans.
- Keep the homepage short: current status, research interests, four destinations, a student-resource prompt and verified links.
- Use prose and ruled lists instead of numbered cards or a visualised career journey.
- Keep the Research page focused on interests and publications. Empty data should remain visibly empty rather than being replaced with invented work.
- Let Bodhi's own teaching philosophy carry the Teaching page.
- Use colour for structure. Mineral blue marks the student prompt and pale section openings; oxblood remains a small link and focus accent.

## Choices deliberately avoided

- Publication counts, testimonials, impact metrics or claims of expertise.
- Patient-facing marketing language or appointment calls to action.
- Stock operating-theatre imagery, surgical instruments or anatomical decoration.
- A separate top-level page for every CV category.
- Large mission statements, taglines, elaborate motion or app-like interfaces.

## Test for future additions

Before adding a page, section or feature, ask whether it helps a visitor find verified work, understand a genuine research interest or use a teaching resource. If the same information fits clearly on an existing page, keep it there.
